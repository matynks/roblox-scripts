## 1 · RAND statistic (chapter 4)

| | Wording |
|---|---|
| **Original (v3)** | "Winners? Companies, customers — and the cost of too little sleep, estimated at up to four hundred and eleven billion dollars a year in the US, **disappears**. Losers? Workers: when everyone can work more, an hour might be worth less." |
| **Revised (v4)** | "Winners? Companies and customers. **In 2016, RAND estimated that lack of sleep cost the US economy up to four hundred and eleven billion dollars a year — in missed work, lower productivity and earlier deaths. Here, that could shrink, though a nonstop world may bring new costs.** Losers? Workers: when everyone can work more, an hour might be worth less." |
| **On screen (new card, same beat)** | *RAND Europe, 2016 · lack of sleep among US workers · up to $411 bn a year* |

**Why:**
* **Attribution:** the figure is now attributed to its source and year, in speech and on screen.
* **What RAND counted:** absences, lower productivity at work, and higher mortality among workers who sleep too little. It is given in RAND's own term, "lack of sleep".
* **"Disappears" is replaced by a hedge.** RAND's comparison is people sleeping 7–9 hours, not a world without sleep, so its model does not carry over. A world that never stops may bring costs RAND never modelled (D05, D06). The hedge is ledger **SP-19**; the figure is **G01**.
* **Timing:** the beat needs 24 s instead of 15 s, so chapter 4 runs 68 s — still within ±15 % of its window.

## 2 · Circadian line (chapter 8)

| | Wording |
|---|---|
| **Original (v3)** | Picture: "They lie in the grass." — "**In the dark, your body clocks finally get a real night.**" |
| **Revised (v4)** | Picture: "They lie in the grass, **awake**." — "**No light up here holds back your body's signal of night. It isn't sleep. It's just night.**" |

**Why:**
* **"Finally get a real night" contradicted the world rule.** It implied their clocks had been broken or deprived, but HR4 says the clock works normally — and a normal clock responds to light.
* **The revision states only what the evidence supports:**
  * light suppresses melatonin (B08);
  * melatonin is the body's signal of night (B06, B07).

  It does **not** claim melatonin is rising at that moment, because ten years of night work may have shifted their clocks (B09).
* **"It isn't sleep" rules out reading darkness as sleep** (HR2, HR5).
* **It calls back chapter 3's lamp test** ("Switch on a light, and it fades").

## 4 · Opening visual — exactly what the audience sees in the first three seconds

**Original (v3):** "**Frame 1 — no black, no silence.** Extreme close-up of Leo's (25) wide-open eye; in its reflection a huge luminous clock reads 3:00 (the thumbnail image). **The camera pulls back through the office glass**: a city at full daytime intensity under a night sky, and a floodlit playground with kids on the swings."

**Revised (v4):**

| Time | Picture | Sound and voice |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00.0 — frame 1** | A **wide shot, already in motion.** A floodlit playground packed with children fills the lower two-thirds of the frame: two swings at the top of their arc, a slide, kids running. Behind it, the night city — navy sky, no stars, every window lit. Centred above the skyline is a huge luminous clock face reading **3:00** (teal and cool white). **Night, 3:00 and a full playground all read at phone size from the first frame.** The camera only pushes in 2 %; there is no pull-back. | Laughter, swing chains, a distant school bell, traffic hum — all from frame 1. |
| 0:00.2–0:03.0 | — | VO: "It's three a.m. — and the playground is full." (it runs over the cut) |
| **0:02.0 — cut** | **The thumbnail shot:** an extreme close-up of Leo's (25) wide-open eyes at an office window, with the 3:00 clock and the floodlit playground reflected in the glass. Hold to 0:03. | — |

**0:03–0:10 also changes:** v3's "Fast cuts: a school…; a traffic jam; **Leo at the window**" becomes "Fast cuts: a school…; a traffic jam."

**Why:**
* In v3 the playground was readable only at the end of a long camera move away from an eye. Now the paradox — night, 3 a.m. and a full playground — is readable in frame 1.
* The thumbnail image still arrives inside the first three seconds (0:02).
* Showing Leo again at 0:03–0:10 repeated a shot seen a second earlier, so that cut is removed.
