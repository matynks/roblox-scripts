**Stage 3 · 2026-11-15 · status: complete for the Story Lock (G2)**

* **What was read:** search summaries of abstracts, agency pages (NIH, NIGMS, CDC, BLS, UN, IARC), society statements (AASM/SRS) and university press releases.

  * **A** — abstract, official page or the authors' own release.

* **How to read it:** each finding carries its evidence class (definitions in `PRODUCTION_STANDARD.md` §5) and source IDs (§4 below). Script wording rules per claim are in [02_CLAIM_LEDGER.md](02_CLAIM_LEDGER.md).

## 1. What the story can rely on — ten takeaways

1. **Sleep fills about a third of life.** — ESTABLISHED (S01)
2. **Two systems time it.**
   * A **homeostatic sleep pressure** builds while you are awake — the "hourglass". Adenosine is a key signal, and caffeine blocks it.
   * A **circadian body clock** in the brain is set by light — the "clock". Melatonin is its night signal.
   * They are **separate mechanisms**, which is exactly why the premise can remove the need for sleep while keeping the clock. — ESTABLISHED / RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S03, S04, S05, S07, S08)
3. **Sleep is linked to memory, immunity and metabolism.** The evidence is strong but mostly from limited studies, so it is narrated as "studies suggest" or "in one study". — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S10, S12, S14–S16)
4. **Nobody knows all of what sleep does.** Its core function is still debated. — UNKNOWN (S24, S25)
5. **REM sleep has no agreed single function.** Dreams happen in both REM and non-REM sleep. — UNKNOWN / RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S20–S23)
6. **Whether sleep "washes" waste from the brain is contested.** A 2013 mouse study said yes; a 2024 mouse study said clearance is *lower* during sleep. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED, contested → UNKNOWN for humans (S17–S19)
7. **Sleep looks ancient and universal.** Even brainless animals (jellyfish, Hydra) show sleep-like states. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S27, S28)
8. **Nature has nudged the dial, never to zero.** A few rare families feel rested on about 4–6 hours. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED, small studies (S30, S31)
9. **More hours ≠ more output or happiness.**
   * Output per hour fell beyond about 49 hours a week (WWI data).
   * Well-being levels off around 2 hours of daily free time and can dip beyond about 5.

   — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S39, S40)
10. **The night already matters beyond sleep.**
    * Lighting uses roughly 15 % of the world's electricity.
    * Over 80 % of people live under light-polluted skies.
    * Artificial light at night changes how animals behave.

    — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED / ESTABLISHED (S46–S49)

## 2. Findings by topic

### 2.1 Why humans need sleep
* Sleep takes about one-third of our time. It has two main types, REM and non-REM (three stages), which cycle through the night. — ESTABLISHED (S01)
* Most adults (18–60) need **at least 7 hours** a night on a regular basis (AASM/SRS consensus). — ESTABLISHED, as a recommendation (S32)
* About **1 in 3 US adults** reported less than 7 hours (CDC, 2014 data; 444,306 adults; 65.2 % got ≥ 7 h). — ESTABLISHED, survey (S33)
* Losing sleep impairs the brain fast:
  * After **17–19 hours awake**, performance on some tests matched or was worse than at a **0.05 % blood-alcohol level** (39 adults; computer tests, not driving).
  * Six hours a night for two weeks produced deficits comparable to **up to two nights** of total sleep loss.

  — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S35, S34)
* Insufficient sleep is estimated to cost the US economy **up to $411 billion a year** (2.28 % of GDP; ~1.2 million working days). — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED, a model estimate (S37)

### 2.2 Memory and learning
* Current theory gives sleep an **active** role. Recently learned memories are reactivated ("replayed") during **slow-wave (deep) sleep** and integrated into long-term memory. The emphasis has shifted from REM to slow-wave sleep. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S10)
* **One leading hypothesis** (synaptic homeostasis): learning while awake strengthens connections, and sleep renormalises them. It is a hypothesis, not settled. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S11)

### 2.3 Immune function
* In a quarantine study of **164 adults** given a cold virus, those who had slept **under 6 hours** (measured by wrist actigraphy) were **4.2× more likely** to develop a cold than those sleeping over 7 hours. Under 5 hours: 4.5×. The result was adjusted for season, weight, income, stress, smoking and more. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S12)
* Vaccine responses:
  * Short sleep around vaccination is linked to weaker antibody responses (flu: small experiment, 2002; hepatitis B: 125 adults, 2012).
  * A 2023 meta-analysis found the link where sleep was **objectively measured**, not when self-reported.

  — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S13–S15)

### 2.4 Metabolism
* **11 healthy young men** spent **4 hours in bed for 6 nights**:
  * glucose tolerance fell (p < 0.02);
  * thyrotropin fell;
  * evening cortisol and sympathetic activity rose.

  The authors compared the effects to normal ageing, and everything recovered with 12-hour nights. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S16)
* **Being awake costs energy.** Seven adults stayed in bed on a controlled diet. A night without sleep raised 24-hour energy use by **about 7 %**; the night's difference was about **135 kcal**. The authors warn this is not a weight-loss strategy. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED, small (S36)

### 2.5 Sleep pressure and adenosine
* In cats, **adenosine** in the basal forebrain rose during waking, kept rising with prolonged waking, and fell slowly during recovery sleep. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S03)
* Adenosine is widely accepted as an endogenous sleep-regulating substance. **Caffeine** acts mainly by **blocking adenosine A1/A2A receptors**. — ESTABLISHED for caffeine; RESEARCH-SUPPORTED for adenosine's exact role (S08)
* The **two-process model** has been the field's main framework for three decades. Homeostatic **Process S** interacts with circadian **Process C**, continuously. — ESTABLISHED as the standard model (S04)

### 2.6 Circadian rhythms
* The master clock is the **suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN)**. Light and dark influence it most; food, stress, activity and temperature also do. It synchronises clocks in organs and controls melatonin from the light the eyes receive. — ESTABLISHED (S05)
* The molecular clock is a feedback loop of clock genes and proteins (PER, TIM) shared by all multicellular organisms. It won the **2017 Nobel Prize** (Hall, Rosbash, Young). — ESTABLISHED (S06)
* **Core body temperature** has a daily rhythm: lowest in the **early-morning hours** (about 0.5 °C below 37 °C), highest in the late afternoon. The exact low point varies between people. — ESTABLISHED in general; only educational sources were found (S09)

### 2.7 Melatonin
* Made mainly by the **pineal gland**, peaking **at night**. Darkness signals via the eyes; light suppresses it; the master clock drives the rhythm. — ESTABLISHED (S07, S05)
* Melatonin is a **darkness / timing signal**, not an off switch. Nothing in the sources supports describing it as "the hormone that puts you to sleep". — wording guardrail

### 2.8 REM and NREM sleep, and dreams
* REM sleep was described in **1953** (Aserinsky & Kleitman, 20 sleeping adults). Awakenings from REM produced far more dream reports. — ESTABLISHED (S02)
* **Dreams occur in both NREM and REM sleep.** A posterior cortical "hot zone" tracks dreaming in both stages; EEG predicted dreaming 92 % of the time and its absence 81 %. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S20)
* **REM's function remains elusive.** Proposals include emotional-memory processing and brain maturation. Some proposed roles (general and procedural memory) now look unlikely; others are inconclusive. — UNKNOWN (S21–S23)

### 2.9 Sleep and brain function, including waste clearance
* **2013, mice:** the space between brain cells was ~60 % larger during natural sleep and anaesthesia, with faster beta-amyloid clearance. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED, one study in mice (S17)
* **2019, humans (13 adults aged 23–33):** slow brain waves during NREM were followed by blood-volume oscillations and **pulses of cerebrospinal fluid**. Waste clearance was not measured. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED, small (S18)
* **2024, mice:** dye injected into brain tissue cleared about **30 % slower in sleep** and 50 % slower under anaesthesia than awake. Some researchers question the methods. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED, contested (S19)
* → **Whether sleep speeds waste clearance in the human brain is unresolved.** — UNKNOWN. Guardrail: never narrate "sleep cleans your brain" as fact.

### 2.10 Scientific unknowns
* There is no agreed single core function of sleep. Proposals range from adaptive inactivity (Siegel 2009) to cellular maintenance; comparative work across species is still looking for it (Anafi et al. 2019). — UNKNOWN (S24, S25)
* Sleep-like states exist in **animals without brains**:
  * the jellyfish *Cassiopea* — quiescence, reduced response, rebound after deprivation;
  * *Hydra* — a nerve net, sleep homeostasis.

  Definitions are debated. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S27, S28)
* **Frigatebirds sleep in flight**, one hemisphere at a time or both, very little and in short bouts. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S29)
* **Natural short sleepers:**
  * DEC2 carriers averaged ~6.25 h vs 8.06 h (as reported).
  * ADRB1 was found in a three-generation family.
  * Coverage says 4–6 hours "without apparent ill effects".

  Small family studies. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED, limited (S30, S31)
* **Not available in the literature:** what humans would be like with **no fatigue at all** (focus, motivation, mood over 20+ waking hours). — UNKNOWN

### 2.11 Society, economy and the city (for chapters 4–7)
* **Work schedules:** in 2017–18, **84 %** of US wage and salary workers had a regular daytime schedule, so ~1 in 6 did not. — ESTABLISHED (S38)
* **Hours vs output:** WWI British munition workers. Output rose in step with hours up to **~49 h/week**, then more slowly; **70 hours produced little more than 56**. Historical, one sector, workers who got tired. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S39)
* **Free time vs well-being:** ATUS data on ~35,000 people plus two experiments show an inverted U. Well-being **levels off around 2 hours** of free time a day and **dips beyond about 5**. The dip shrinks when the time is spent productively. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED, correlational plus experiments (S40)
* **Social time:**
  * US social engagement with friends, family outside the home and others fell between 2003 and 2020, steepest for the young (S41).
  * The share of free time spent with people from other households fell from 21.9 % (2003) to 17.3 % (2019) (S42).

  — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED
* **Right to disconnect:** France was first (2016). Australia's law took effect in **August 2024** and lets employees refuse unreasonable after-hours contact. Portugal, Spain, Belgium and Ontario are also cited. — ESTABLISHED (S43)
* **Night work and health:** IARC classifies night shift work as **Group 2A, "probably carcinogenic"** (2019). Human evidence is limited, and the class reflects the strength of evidence, not the size of risk. — ESTABLISHED as a classification (S44). *Not planned for the story* (see the ledger, E03).
* **Transit:** the New York City subway runs **24/7/365**, with lighter late-night service and maintenance reroutes. — ESTABLISHED (S45)
* **Energy:** lighting uses roughly **15 %** of global electricity (UNEP; another UNEP page says 15–19 % of energy). Solar panels produce nothing at night (physics). — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED estimate / ESTABLISHED
* **Light pollution:**
  * **More than 80 %** of the world's people, and over 99 % in the US and Europe, live under light-polluted skies. The Milky Way is hidden from over a third of humanity (S47).
  * Citizen scientists measured **+9.6 % sky brightness per year** from 2011 to 2022. At that rate brightness quadruples in 18 years, so 250 visible stars become ~100 (S48).
* **Wildlife:** a meta-analysis of artificial light at night found strong effects on hormone levels, the daily start of activity, life-history traits, predation, cognition and **sea-turtle sea-finding**. Birds sing and forage earlier. — RESEARCH-SUPPORTED (S49)
* **Dark-sky places:** Flagstaff, Arizona was the **first International Dark Sky City (2001)**. About **250 certified dark-sky places** were reported by late 2025. — ESTABLISHED (S50)
* **Population:** ~**8.2 billion** people (2024); global life expectancy **73.3 years** (2024). — ESTABLISHED (S51)

## 3. What the breakthrough would have to change (analysis for Chapter 2)

This is reasoning from the findings above, not science about a real mechanism.

1. **Empty the hourglass for good.** No sleep pressure, ever: whatever adenosine and the other pressure signals do, they never add up to sleepiness. Real biology never does this — even the rarest short sleepers need hours of sleep.
2. **Do sleep's work while awake.**
   * Known work: memory consolidation, immune and metabolic regulation.
   * Unknown work: whatever else sleep does that science has not identified (UNKNOWN, S24, S25).

   The premise must cover both, or people would be harmed — and the brief rules harm out.
3. **Leave the clock alone.** The brief requires the circadian system to stay. So melatonin still rises at night, body temperature still dips in the early morning, and light still sets the clock. People notice night; they are simply not tired.
4. **Logical consequence: no dreams.** Dreams are experiences during sleep, so a world without sleep has no dreams. What that would do to people is UNKNOWN, because the function of dreaming and REM is unknown.
5. **Energy bill.** Waking costs more energy than sleeping (S36), so bodies awake 24 hours would eat more. The size of this at population scale is a SPECULATIVE extrapolation.
