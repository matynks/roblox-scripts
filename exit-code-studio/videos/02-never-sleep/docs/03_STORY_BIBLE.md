## 0. The story in one line

One ordinary Tuesday, humanity stops needing sleep. Over ten years, eight billion people discover that the hours they got back were never the gift — deciding what those hours are for is.

## 1. The thought experiment

### What stays real
Sleep is timed by two **separate** systems (B01). We call them the **hourglass** and the **clock** — our metaphors, not scientific terms.

* **The hourglass — sleep pressure.** It builds the longer you're awake; adenosine is one of its signals, and caffeine blocks it (B02, B03).
* **The clock — the circadian system.**
  * A small cluster of nerve cells deep in the brain keeps time for the whole body (B04).
  * On its own it runs a little over 24 hours, so light reaching the eyes resets it every day (B05).
  * In darkness it has the **pineal gland** release melatonin, the body's signal of night (B06, B07).
  * **Light at night suppresses melatonin and can shift the clock** (B08, B09).

### The rules of this world (HYPOTHETICAL ASSUMPTION — ledger HR1–HR8)
1. **Moment zero:** one ordinary **Monday night in September** is the last time anyone sleeps. Nobody notices until Tuesday night. From then on, **nobody needs sleep**, including babies born afterwards.
2. **Nobody ever gets tired** from being awake. The hourglass never fills.
3. **Not sleeping causes no harm.** Whatever sleep did — known and unknown — now happens while awake.
4. **The clock works exactly as it does today** — reset by light, melatonin in darkness, suppressed and shifted by light at night. **The only change: none of it makes anyone sleepy.**
5. **Nobody dreams.** This is a **rule we chose**, not a scientific prediction. Science doesn't know what dreams are for, or whether dreaming could exist without sleep (D03). It is stated on screen as a rule (chapter 2).
6. People can still lie down, rest and close their eyes.
7. Everything else is unchanged: physics, the sun, human nature.
8. **How it happens is never explained.**

**The device:** the breakthrough stops the hourglass and leaves the clock running. The need for sleep disappears; **the biological night does not.** This distinction runs through the whole story and pays off at the end, when the characters find a real night again.

## 2. Theme, tone and style

* **Theme:** time doesn't make a life; choices do. Sleep was the one pause everybody shared and nobody had to justify. Without it, every pause has to be chosen.
* **Tone:** a cinematic science documentary with a human heart — wonder, then consequences, then a quiet, earned ending.
  * **Balanced:** the breakthrough brings real gains — learning, care, creativity, prosperity, and Mia's education, which is paid for by night work — alongside real costs.
* **Escalation** (BRIEF): one family (ch. 3) → the world economy (4) → the city (5) → people (6) → civilisation (7) → one person again (8).
* **Style:** Video #1's look, with the navy science-graphic style for chapter 2.
* **Colour script:**
  * **The 24-hour world:** navy/black with restrained teal and cool white (the thumbnail palette).
  * **Home:** warm amber pockets.
  * **BLACK 24:** grey and cold teal neon.
  * **The ending:** deep blue-black with silver stars.
* **Point of view:** second person, "you" = Leo, as in Video #1. The hook's opening "your life" speaks to the viewer; the final question turns back to the viewer.

## 8. Retention plan

* **Immediate payoff:** the first frame *is* the thumbnail promise (eyes open, the 3:00 clock, the night city).
* **Open loops always running:** L1–L8. The notebook drawing stays open until the last minute.
* **New information in every chapter:**
  * 2: sleep science, memory, immunity, metabolism, the unknowns.
  * 3: melatonin and light.
  * 4: the scale, the economy, productivity research.
  * 5: transit, maintenance, energy, the sky.
  * 6: happiness research, the right to disconnect.
  * 7: the world at scale.
* **A visual change at least every ~4 s** (Production Standard §8); every chapter has its own set or graphic language.
* **Transitions** are single bridging lines, not recaps.
* **No artificial suspense:** every promise in the hook is kept, in order.
* **No repetition:** the signature line is said once; chapter 8 only echoes it.
* **Emotional payoff:** set up in chapter 3 ("When I have time"), kept alive in chapters 6–7 ("Next week", 👍), and paid off in chapter 8 with almost no narration — the caption, the empty chair, the drawing against the sky, "Goodnight, Mia." One quiet minute after six minutes of acceleration.

## 9. Science guardrails for the script

* Every factual sentence has a claim ID, and the wording follows its class (ledger v2).
* **The two systems stay separate:** the hourglass stops; the clock keeps working, and responds to light.
* **Never say:**
  * that the clock releases melatonin (the pineal gland does);
  * that light switches melatonin off (it suppresses it);
  * that melatonin is the sleep hormone;
  * that sleep is "unconscious" or "switching off";
  * that scientists know every purpose of sleep;
  * that REM has one function;
  * that sleep cleans the brain, as a fact;
  * that "no dreams" is anything other than a rule of this world.
* **No scaling up** of small-study results to population numbers. The four-billion comparison is shown with its assumption.
* **Effect sizes and statistics (v3):**
  * an odds ratio is said as "odds", never "times as likely";
  * every statistic keeps its construct ("too little sleep", not "tiredness"), its year and its scope (lighting: IEA 2024, buildings plus outdoor);
  * no emphasis words ("alone", "just") that the source doesn't support.
* **Chapter 7 is imagined, never predicted.** Chapter 8 says plainly what real people need.
